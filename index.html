About & education

TANISHA BIYANI M.Sc. Geoinformatics · BVIEER, Pune

I'm a postgraduate student in M.Sc. Geoinformatics, working at the intersection of Geographic Information Systems and remote sensing to make sense of real-world spatial problems — from shrinking lakes to growing cities.

My path from a B.A. in Geography to an M.Sc. in Geoinformatics reflects a move from theory toward practice: using satellite imagery and geospatial tools to visualise change, forecast risk, and support better decisions on the ground.

I'm especially drawn to hydrological modelling and watershed management, the socio-economic side of urban green space, and building Web GIS applications that put spatial data in front of the people who need it.

Field research & surveys

Ground-truthing satellite work with transects, GPS surveys, and community data collection.

Pashan Lake

The Pashan Lake survey was key to understanding the integration of field data with GIS and remote sensing for environmental monitoring. I learned firsthand how to spatially analyze the impact of invasive species, such as water hyacinth, on lake biodiversity. The project sharpened my skills in data interpretation and temporal mapping, confirming that geospatial monitoring is vital for informing sustainable management and conservation strategies for vulnerable water ecosystems.

Mayureshwar Supe

In Mayureshwar Supe, which was a dry scrubland, I achieved hands-on expertise in vegetation sampling using line and belt transects, mastering the calculation of species density and abundance. A key outcome was understanding and performing ground truthing—relating field data directly to NDVI values from remote sensing—to ensure accurate vegetation analysis. The exercise significantly improved my skills in data collection, spatial interpretation, and integrating field ecology with satellite observations for effective ecological monitoring.

Mulshi

Field visit to Mulshi was a comprehensive exercise in ecological data collection and analysis. The practical work involved establishing line transects to survey vegetation, and using a densometer to quantify canopy cover. Furthermore, I utilised modern mobile technology, including GPS Essentials for accurate geospatial recording of locations and the Epicollect forms platform for standardised digital data entry. The combination of these techniques allowed for robust ecological sampling, which was supplemented by the crucial task of collecting data on ecosystem services provided by the Mulshi environment, thus connecting the biological observations to their broader socio-economic and environmental value.
